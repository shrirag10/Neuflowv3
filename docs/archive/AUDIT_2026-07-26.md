# NeuFlow v3 — full critical audit

Line-by-line review of the algorithm, the training code, and whether the
approach can work. Every claim below is either proved algebraically or measured;
where I previously asserted something that turns out to be wrong, that is called
out explicitly.

---

## Part 1 — What is actually correct

Verified, not assumed:

1. **The conv reformulation of window projection is mathematically exact.**
   `Linear(concat_j F(p+off_j))` vs `bilinear_sample(conv_W(F))(p)`. Both are
   linear in `F`; because the offsets `off_j` are *integer* in grid units,
   `floor(p+off_j) = floor(p)+off_j` and the bilinear weights are identical for
   every `j`, so the two sums commute. PyTorch's `conv2d` is cross-correlation
   (no kernel flip), which is the orientation the window gather uses, and
   `F.pad(mode='replicate')` matches `grid_sample(padding_mode='border')`.
   Confirmed empirically earlier: EPE identical to 4 decimals.
2. **Convex head is genuinely bounded.** Output is a convex combination of
   existing flow values, so it cannot overshoot — this really did fix the
   original unbounded-regression failure.
3. **Zero-init reproduces bilinear.** Verified numerically (max diff 0.00000 px).
4. **Gradient freezing works.** 0 of the learned backbone weights changed over
   30K steps.
5. **GT supervision is exact.** With `--no_query_jitter` (used in all HPC runs)
   queries land on integer pixels, so the `align_corners=False` normalization
   maps to exact pixel centers and `grid_sample` returns the true GT with no
   interpolation across motion boundaries or invalid pixels.
6. **The Laplace NLL is correct.** For 2-D independent Laplace,
   $-\log p = (|e_x|+|e_y|)/b + 2\log b + \text{const}$, which is what the code
   computes; `b = exp(clamp(x,-6,6))` gives `b=1` at zero-init.
7. **Multi-scale supervision matches RAFT** (`gamma^(n-i-1)`), and the decoder is
   supervised at every s8 iteration, so it does see 3-iteration coarse flow
   during training.
8. **Sparse == dense** at the same coordinates. Mechanical property, holds.

## Part 2 — Bugs found

### 2.1 The "frozen" backbone is not frozen (measured)

`my_freeze_model()` only sets `requires_grad=False`. It never puts BatchNorm in
eval mode, and `model.train()` runs every epoch. BatchNorm updates
`running_mean`/`running_var` on **every forward pass in train mode regardless of
`requires_grad`**.

Measured on `chairs_v2dev/step_030000` vs `neuflow_mixed.pth`:

| Quantity | Change |
|---|---|
| learned backbone weights changed | **0** (freezing works) |
| mean drift in `running_mean` | **7.4 %** |
| mean drift in `running_var` | **17.4 %** |
| `num_batches_tracked` | 135,500 → 165,500 (**+30,000 = one per step**) |

Consequence, measured by restoring only the BN buffers to v2's values and
re-running the coarse pass on the same image pair:

```
coarse flow magnitude        15.93 px
mean |Δflow| from BN drift    0.350 px      <-- 7x the entire v3-vs-v2 gap (0.049 px)
max  |Δflow| from BN drift    5.857 px
```

So the coarse flow that v3's decoder consumes is **not** the coarse flow v2
produces. The comparison was never "same front end, different upsampler". The
backbone silently domain-adapted to the training data, and some unknown part of
v3's apparent advantage may be that adaptation rather than the decoder.

**Fix:** set backbone BN modules to `.eval()` during training (or
`track_running_stats=False`). Then either re-run, or deliberately keep BN
adaptation *and* give v2 the same treatment before comparing.

### 2.2 I was wrong to dismiss the BN drift in the merge script

When `merge_distill_decoder.py` aborted on BN buffer differences I logged it as
"expected, harmless PyTorch behavior, not a real incompatibility" and disabled
the check. That was wrong. `refine_s8` in `distill3` was trained under
distill3's drifted BN statistics; splicing it onto big18's backbone gives it
different input statistics than it was tuned for. **The end-to-end distillation
number (2.398 px) is therefore pessimistic for a reason I introduced.** The
qualitative conclusion (isolated gains overstate deployable gains) still holds,
but that specific number is not a clean measurement.

### 2.3 The decoder never sees the full-resolution image

`ImplicitFlowDecoder.forward(self, img, ...)` takes `img` and uses it **only for
`img.device`** (lines 377–378). It is never read as data. `forward_dense_fast`
does not even accept it.

### 2.4 Train/inference mismatch in fusion

Training uses `forward()` (fusion evaluated per query); evaluation uses
`forward_dense_fast(fusion_on_grid=True)` (fusion evaluated once on the 1/8 grid
and sampled). Costs ~0.02 px. Small, but the models are not evaluated in the
regime they were trained in.

## Part 3 — Does the algorithm work? The ceiling analysis

This is the part that matters. Measured on VKITTI2 Scene18 (256×512 crop),
chairs-trained checkpoint, comparing what the head *achieves* against what its
own candidate set *allows*:

| | EPE |
|---|---|
| bilinear upsampling (no decoder at all) | 1.540 px |
| **trained convex head (actual)** | **1.497 px** |
| oracle: best single candidate of the 10 | **0.881 px** |

**The trained decoder beats plain bilinear by 0.043 px, while simply picking the
best of its own 10 candidates would gain 0.659 px.** It is capturing ~6 % of the
headroom its architecture already has.

### Why? Not what I guessed

My first hypothesis was softmax saturation: `convex_prior[bilinear] = 9.0`
puts 0.9989 of the weight on bilinear at init, deep in the saturated regime
where gradients vanish. **The data refutes this.** The trained head produces:

```
logit spread (before prior): min -38.85  max 10.09  std 3.69
weight on bilinear candidate: mean 0.083, median 0.017
fraction of pixels with bilinear weight > 0.90: 0.0 %
```

The head escaped the prior completely and makes confident, high-magnitude
choices. It is not stuck. It is **confidently choosing badly.**

### The real cause: the head cannot see what it must decide

The decision is a *within-cell* decision, and the head's evidence is nearly
constant within a cell. Measured as within-8×8-cell variance as a fraction of
total variance:

| Quantity | Within-cell share |
|---|---|
| the head's fused feature evidence | **23.3 %** |
| full-resolution image pixels | **35.0 %** |
| the oracle's correct candidate choice | **63.4 %** |

And **96.9 % of 8×8 cells require different candidates for different pixels
inside that same cell.**

(Caveat: the 63.4 % figure is inflated by argmin noise among near-tied
candidates, so treat it as an upper bound. The ordering and the 96.9 % figure
are robust.)

### The asymmetry with v2

v2's upsampler is fed `conv_s8(img0)` — a `kernel=8, stride=8` convolution on the
**raw full-resolution image**, so every 1/8 cell's descriptor encodes the actual
8×8 block of original pixels. From it, v2 predicts `8²×9 = 576` values per cell:
**a separate 9-way convex weighting for each of the 64 sub-pixels**, conditioned
on real high-frequency image content.

v3 predicts one convex weighting per query from features that barely vary inside
the cell. The queryable decoder — the thing meant to be *more* expressive — is
strictly **less informed** for the sub-pixel decision than the fixed upsampler
it replaced.

This single fact explains three separate observations that previously looked
unrelated:

- **the 1 px accuracy gap** (77.6 % vs 69.7 %) — precision is exactly the
  within-cell decision;
- **the Fourier PE null result** — PE supplies *position* within the cell, but
  position is useless without high-frequency *evidence* to locate the boundary;
- **the distillation failure** — a convex combination of a degraded coarse field
  is still degraded; the head cannot repair what is not in its candidates.

## Part 4 — What to do, ranked

### Tier 1 — attacks the root cause

**A. Give the decoder full-resolution image evidence.** Add a small full-res
stem (v2 already pays for exactly this: one `k=8,s=8` conv) and sample it at the
query coordinate. Because it is sampled *at the query*, sub-pixel positions get
genuinely different evidence for the first time — which is precisely what the
implicit formulation was supposed to enable and currently does not. The `img`
argument is already plumbed through to the decoder; it just needs to be used.
Expected to be the single largest accuracy change available, and it directly
targets the metric v3 loses on.

**B. Fix the BatchNorm freeze**, then re-run. Without this, no v3-vs-v2
comparison means what it claims to mean, and no two runs are comparable to each
other.

### Tier 2 — architecture

**C. Break the convex-hull ceiling, safely.** All candidates come from an s8
field, so within a smooth region no convex combination can be more precise than
that field. Add a *bounded* residual on top of the convex blend — e.g.
`flow = convex_blend + tanh(r) * 1px`. This restores sub-pixel expressiveness
without returning to the unbounded regression that failed originally.

**D. 5×5 candidate window.** 25+1 candidates instead of 9+1. One-line change,
more coverage at boundaries; cheap to test.

### Tier 3 — training

**E. Weight the loss toward boundaries.** Almost all available gain is at motion
discontinuities; uniform sampling spends most of its supervision where bilinear
is already optimal.

**F. Remove the fusion train/eval mismatch** — train with `fusion_on_grid=True`
if that is how you evaluate.

## Part 5 — Should NeuFlow v2 be unfrozen?

**Not first, and not as the main lever.**

The measured bottleneck is *information*, not capacity: the s8 feature map is an
8× downsample and physically cannot carry 64 distinct sub-pixel decisions per
cell in a smooth field. Unfreezing asks the backbone to encode high-frequency
detail into a low-resolution map — fighting the architecture — whereas a full-res
stem (Tier 1A) supplies the same information directly, at roughly the cost v2
already pays, with no retraining risk. You also already observed divergence when
unfreezing at batch 4.

If you do unfreeze, do it **after** 1A and 1B, and:
- unfreeze only the s8 branch and `refine_s8`, lr ≈ 1e-5, decoder converged first;
- BN in eval mode regardless (Part 2.1);
- **retrain v2 identically**, or the comparison is meaningless again.

---

## Part 6 — Honest position on the thesis

The queryable/caching/uncertainty story is solid and unique: repeat queries at
2.6 ms against v2's 19.6 ms full recompute, exact sparse-dense equivalence,
arbitrary resolution, and a calibrated per-query confidence v2 structurally
cannot produce. None of that depends on winning at EPE.

The EPE story is currently a coin-flip riding on confounds (leak, BN drift,
mismatched training sets) and should not be the headline until Tier 1 is done
and the runs are repeated. Reframing the contribution around capability rather
than a 0.05 px margin is both more honest and more defensible.
